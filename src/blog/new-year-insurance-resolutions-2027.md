A new year is a good excuse to look at the things you have been putting off. For most people, insurance is near the top of that list. You signed up for a policy, set up autopay, and stopped thinking about it. That is understandable, but it can cost you.

Your life changes. Your coverage should keep up. Here are five insurance resolutions worth making for 2027, whether you are a Kentucky family trying to keep costs down or a small business owner making sure you are actually protected.

## Resolution 1: Review your current coverage limits

Pull out your declarations page for every active policy. If you have not looked at it since you signed up, now is the time. You are looking for a few things:

- **Dwelling coverage on your home policy.** Construction costs have risen significantly over the past few years. If your dwelling limit has not kept pace, you could be underinsured if you need to rebuild. A policy that was adequate in 2023 may fall short in 2027.
- **Liability limits on your auto and home policies.** The minimum liability limits in Kentucky are $25,000 per person for bodily injury on auto. That does not go far in a serious accident. If your income or assets have grown, your liability exposure has grown too.
- **Business coverage limits.** Revenue growth is a good problem to have, but your general liability and commercial property limits should reflect your current operations, not what they looked like when you started.

If you are not sure [how to read your declarations page](/blog/understanding-your-insurance-declarations-page.html), it is worth learning. That document is the single best summary of what you actually have.

Insurance rates change every year. Carriers adjust their pricing based on claims data, reinsurance costs, and market conditions. The carrier that gave you the best rate three years ago may not be the best option today.

## Resolution 3: Close your coverage gaps

There are a few coverage gaps that are more common than they should be:

**No umbrella policy.** If you own a home, have savings, or earn a solid income, a $1 million umbrella policy is one of the best values in insurance. It typically costs $200 to $400 per year and adds an extra layer of liability protection above your auto and home policies.

**No flood insurance.** Standard homeowners policies do not cover flood damage. Period. If you are in a flood-prone area of Kentucky, especially along the Ohio River or in low-lying areas, a separate flood policy is worth the conversation.

**Gaps between personal and business coverage.** If you use your personal vehicle for business, your personal auto policy may not cover an accident that happens during a work trip. If you run a business from home, your homeowners policy probably does not cover business equipment or liability. These gaps are fixable, but only if you address them.

## Resolution 4: Update your inventory and documentation

If you had to file a claim tomorrow, could you prove what you own? A home inventory does not have to be complicated. Walk through your house with your phone and record a video of each room. Open closets and drawers. Note high-value items like electronics, jewelry, and tools.

Store the video somewhere outside your home, whether that is cloud storage, a family member's house, or a safe deposit box. Do the same for important documents: your policy declarations, receipts for major purchases, and photos of your vehicles.

For business owners, document your equipment, inventory, and any improvements you have made to your workspace. This documentation speeds up the claims process and helps you get a fair settlement.

## Resolution 5: Schedule a coverage review

The easiest resolution on this list is also the most effective. Sit down with your agent and walk through everything. A good coverage review takes 20 to 30 minutes and covers:

- Changes in your household (new drivers, renovations, a home-based business)
- Changes in your business (new employees, new services, new locations)
- Discount opportunities you might be missing (bundling, claims-free discounts, safety features)
- Upcoming life events (buying a home, adding a teen driver, retiring)

## What has changed in the insurance market heading into 2027

A few trends are worth knowing about as you plan for the year ahead:

**Home insurance rates are still climbing in many areas.** Severe weather losses across the country have pushed up reinsurance costs, and those costs get passed to policyholders. Shopping your coverage is more important than ever.

**Auto insurance rates stabilized somewhat in late 2026.** After several years of sharp increases driven by higher repair costs and supply chain issues, the rate of increase has slowed. That said, your individual rate depends on your driving record, vehicle, and where you live.

**Cyber liability is becoming a standard conversation for small businesses.** If your business handles customer data, processes payments, or relies on technology, cyber coverage is no longer a nice-to-have.

### FAQ: How often should I review my insurance coverage?

At least once a year, and any time you experience a major life change such as buying a home, getting married, adding a teen driver, starting a business, or making significant purchases. Annual reviews help catch coverage gaps and savings opportunities.

### FAQ: Does shopping my insurance rate affect my credit score?

No. Insurance companies use what is called a "soft pull" when checking your credit for rating purposes. Soft pulls do not affect your credit score. This is different from a hard inquiry, which is what happens when you apply for a loan or credit card.
