Plumbing contractor insurance in Kentucky starts with a state minimum: a plumber must carry at least $250,000 of general liability insurance and show proof of workers' compensation and unemployment insurance compliance, under [KRS 318.030](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=31186). The master plumber license is not valid without that proof on file. That is the floor, not the finish line, and most plumbing claims are bigger than the floor.

This guide covers what the law requires, what it leaves out, and the coverages a working plumbing business should look at beyond the minimum.

## What insurance does Kentucky require for a plumbing license?

The requirement is in the plumbing licensing statute, KRS 318.030, titled "License and general liability insurance required." Subsection (2)(a) says no person, firm, or corporation shall engage in plumbing "unless the person, firm, or corporation maintains general liability insurance in an amount not less than two hundred fifty thousand dollars ($250,000) and submits proof of compliance with workers' compensation and unemployment insurance laws of the Commonwealth."

Three more details in the same subsection matter in practice ([KRS 318.030](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=31186)):

- **Proof comes first.** Proof of insurance goes to the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction (DHBC) before the active master plumber license is issued or renewed.
- **No insurance, no valid license.** The statute says no license is valid without the required insurance, and insurance carriers must notify the department when a licensee's coverage is cancelled.
- **Employees are exempt.** The insurance requirement does not apply to an employee of a person, firm, or corporation engaged in plumbing.

The licensing rule, 815 KAR 20:030, repeats the requirement at renewal. A master plumber renewing must send the department the renewal fee, proof of continuing education, a completed Form DHBC L-1, and "proof of insurance as required by KRS 318.030 for a master plumber" ([815 KAR 20:030, Section 4](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/kar/titles/815/020/030/)).

> **Important:** You may see 815 KAR 20:080 cited online as the source of the $250,000 figure. It is not. That regulation is a table of minimum waste pipe sizes in the Kentucky State Plumbing Code ([815 KAR 20:080](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/kar/titles/815/020/080/)). The insurance requirement lives in KRS Chapter 318.

## Is a surety bond required for a Kentucky plumber?

We did not find one. KRS 318.030 requires liability insurance and proof of workers' comp and unemployment insurance compliance, and the renewal list in 815 KAR 20:030 names a fee, continuing education, insurance proof and a form. Neither mentions a surety bond for the state plumbing license.

That does not mean you will never need a bond. A bond is a three-party promise that guarantees you will finish a job or meet an obligation, which is different from insurance that pays for a loss. If a contract, a project owner or a city or county asks for one, that is a separate request. Our page on [commercial bonds](/commercial/bonds.html) explains the difference, and your contract will say which kind it wants.

## How much general liability does a plumber really need?

The state minimum is $250,000, and the statute says "not less than," so higher limits are allowed and common. Whether $250,000 is enough depends on the work. A burst supply line in a finished basement can run through that limit on drywall, flooring and cabinetry alone, before you count anyone's medical bills or a lawsuit.

Contracts usually decide the real number. General contractors, builders and commercial customers often write required limits and certificates of insurance into the agreement, and those limits can sit well above the state floor. Read the insurance clause before you bid, not after you win the job. Our [general liability](/commercial/general-liability.html) page shows what the policy covers.

A few policy details worth checking with your agent:

- **Per-occurrence and aggregate limits.** The statute states one number. Policies have a limit per claim and a total for the year, and a busy plumber can use up the second one.
- **Subcontractor requirements.** If you hire subs, ask for their certificates. If you are a sub, expect to hand yours over.
- **Exclusions.** Some policies written for other trades exclude plumbing work or limit it. Ask to see the exclusions, not only the declarations page.

## What is completed operations coverage, and why do plumbers need it?

Completed operations coverage pays for claims that arise after you finish a job and leave. For plumbers, this is where the expensive claims live. A fitting that was not fully seated, a poorly soldered joint or a supply line that fails months later can leak inside a wall for weeks before anyone sees it.

New construction makes this real in growing areas like Bullitt County: a connection that fails after the first owner moves in can lead to a claim against the plumber long after the final inspection.

Check two things on your policy. First, confirm completed operations is included and not excluded. Second, ask how long coverage lasts after the policy ends if you change carriers, because some claims surface after the policy that was in force when you did the work has ended.

## Does general liability cover water damage from plumbing work?

Often, but not always, and this is where claims get denied. General liability generally responds when your work damages someone else's property, but policies carry exclusions that can cut into water claims. Some exclude your own faulty workmanship, which means the cost to redo the bad pipe may not be covered even if the resulting damage is.

Ask your agent three direct questions:

1. Does the policy exclude water damage or mold, or cap it at a lower limit?
2. Does it exclude "your work" after completion, and does a completed operations grant fix that?
3. Does it carve out any plumbing activity, such as repiping, backflow or gas lines?

Freeze-thaw season makes the questions practical. Pipes burst in cold snaps along the Ohio River in Owensboro and across Bullitt County, and emergency calls pile up. Fast work on a frozen line is exactly when mistakes happen, so know how your policy treats it before the phone starts ringing.

## Is workers comp required for plumbing contractors in Kentucky?

If you have employees, yes. Kentucky law says every employer under the workers' compensation chapter must either insure its liability for compensation with an authorized carrier or furnish proof of its financial ability to pay directly ([KRS 342.340](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/Statutes/statute.aspx?id=43318)). The plumbing license statute ties into this, because KRS 318.030 asks for proof of compliance with workers' compensation laws.

Plumbing is physical work: trenches, crawlspaces, ladders, torches and heavy fixtures. Classification matters, so make sure your payroll is coded to the right job duties. Our statewide guide to [workers compensation in Kentucky](/blog/workers-comp-requirements-kentucky-2026) covers exemptions and penalties, and our [workers compensation](/commercial/workers-compensation.html) page covers coverage.

## What other coverage should a Kentucky plumber consider?

The statute sets the minimum for the license. These coverages protect the rest of the business:

- **Commercial auto.** A personal auto policy generally will not cover a loaded work van. Add hired and non-owned coverage if employees ever drive their own vehicles on the job.
- **Inland marine (tools and equipment).** Drain machines, pipe threaders, press tools and cameras travel in vans and sit on job sites. Inland marine coverage follows them, where standard property coverage usually stays at your shop.
- **Commercial property.** If you rent or own a shop and keep inventory, cover the building contents.
- **Umbrella liability.** It adds limits on top of your general liability and auto, which helps when a contract asks for more than the base policy offers.

Many of these overlap with the trades next door. ## How does insurance affect my DHBC license?

Directly. Because KRS 318.030(2)(c) says no license is valid without the required insurance, a lapse in coverage puts your license at risk, and your carrier must tell the department when it cancels your policy. Renew your policy on time, and send your agent the DHBC renewal paperwork early so the certificate is ready when you file.

### FAQ: How much general liability insurance does a Kentucky plumber need?

At least $250,000 under KRS 318.030(2)(a), along with proof of workers' compensation and unemployment insurance compliance. Contracts with builders and commercial customers often ask for more, so check each agreement.

### FAQ: Does the 815 KAR 20:080 regulation set the plumber insurance minimum?

No. 815 KAR 20:080 is a table of minimum waste pipe sizes. The insurance minimum is in KRS 318.030, and 815 KAR 20:030 requires proof of it when a master plumber renews.

### FAQ: Do employees of a plumbing company need their own liability policy?

No. KRS 318.030(2)(d) says the insurance requirement does not apply to an employee of a person, firm, or corporation engaged in plumbing. The business carries the policy.

### FAQ: What does a surety bond cover that insurance does not?

A bond guarantees that you will fulfill a contract or obligation, and if you do not, the bond company can pay the other party and then look to you for repayment. Insurance pays for covered losses. We did not find a bond requirement for the state plumbing license.

### FAQ: Is workers comp required for a plumber with no employees?

Workers' compensation duties under KRS 342.340 apply to employers. A plumber working alone with no employees is in a different position, but contracts and general contractors may still ask for proof. Talk it through with an agent before you assume.
