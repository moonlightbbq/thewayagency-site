Workers compensation is one of those things business owners know they probably need but rarely understand until something goes wrong. In Kentucky, the rules are straightforward but the consequences of getting them wrong are serious. Here is what employers need to know heading into 2026.

## Who needs workers comp in Kentucky

Kentucky law is clear on this: if you have one or more employees, you are generally required to carry [workers compensation insurance](/commercial/workers-compensation.html). That threshold is lower than many states, and it catches a lot of small businesses off guard.

Under [KRS Chapter 342](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/chapter.aspx?id=38914), the requirement applies regardless of whether your employees are full-time, part-time, or seasonal. It does not matter how small your operation is. A restaurant owner with one dishwasher, a [contractor](/industries/general-contractors) with one laborer, a retailer with one cashier — they all need coverage.

Corporate executive officers count as employees by default ([KRS 342.640(2)](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=43049)); an officer can elect not to be covered under the commissioner's administrative regulations ([KRS 342.650(6)](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=51336)). Owners work the other way: sole proprietors, and partners or LLC members who share in the profits and decisions, are covered only if they elect coverage by an endorsement on a workers' comp policy ([KRS 342.012](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=44523)). That election rule does not reduce the business's duty to cover its employees or its nonqualified partners or members, meaning those paid for their work without a share of profits or decisions ([KRS 342.012(1), (4)](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=44523)).

## Exemptions from Kentucky workers comp requirements

There are some narrow exemptions, and business owners often assume they qualify when they do not. The most common legitimate exemptions include:

- **Sole proprietors with no employees.** If you truly work alone and have no one on your payroll, you are not required to carry workers comp. The moment you hire someone, you need a policy. You can still elect coverage for yourself by endorsement ([KRS 342.012](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=44523)).
- **Agricultural employees.** Anyone "employed in agriculture" is exempt ([KRS 342.650(5)](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=51336)). Agriculture is defined by the work, not the size of the farm: operating farm premises, growing and harvesting crops, raising livestock and poultry, and work incidental to it. Commercial processing, packing, drying, storing or canning for market is not agriculture ([KRS 342.0011(18)](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=56771)).
- **Direct sellers** as defined in section 3508(b)(2) of the Internal Revenue Code ([KRS 342.650(11)](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=51336)).
- **Domestic workers** in a private home, when the household has fewer than two domestic employees who each regularly work 40 or more hours a week ([KRS 342.650(1)](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=51336)).

A critical point: simply calling someone an independent contractor does not make them one. Kentucky, like most states, looks at the actual working relationship. If you control when, where, and how someone does their work, they are likely an employee regardless of what your contract says. Misclassifying workers to avoid workers comp obligations is one of the fastest ways to create legal and financial problems for yourself.

## Contractors and subcontractors

In Kentucky, any contractor or subcontractor with even one employee must carry workers' comp ([KRS 342.630](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=32533)). If a subcontractor hasn't secured coverage, the contractor that hired it, and that contractor's insurer, are liable for the subcontractor's injured employees and can recover the cost from the sub ([KRS 342.610(2)](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=47623)). That is why general contractors collect a certificate of insurance from every sub before work starts. Before a building permit is issued, the local building official needs either proof of coverage or a written exemption certification on the commissioner's form ([KRS 342.610(6)](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=47623)).

## Penalties for non-compliance

Kentucky law sets these penalties for going without required coverage ([KRS 342.990](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=52607)):

- **Civil fines** of $100 to $1,000 per offense. For an employer without coverage, each employee and each day is a separate offense ([KRS 342.990(7)(c)](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=52607)).
- **Criminal penalties:** a fine of $100 to $1,000, 30 to 180 days in jail, or both, for each offense ([KRS 342.990(9)(a)](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=52607)).
- **Personal liability for the penalties** for an owner, partner, LLC principal or corporate officer who knowingly authorized or carried out the violation ([KRS 342.990(9)(c)](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=52607)).
- **A court order to stop operating.** The commissioner can ask Franklin Circuit Court to restrain or enjoin an employer that is out of compliance ([KRS 342.402](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=32497)).

The cost of a workers comp policy is almost always a fraction of what non-compliance would cost you.

## How workers comp rates work

Workers compensation premiums are not arbitrary. They are calculated using a specific formula, and understanding it gives you some control over what you pay.

**Classification codes.** Every job function is assigned a classification code by the National Council on Compensation Insurance (NCCI). Office workers have low-risk codes with low rates. Roofers, electricians, and [restaurant workers](/industries/restaurants) have higher-risk codes with higher rates. Your premium starts with the rate assigned to each classification code on your policy.

**Payroll.** Rates are applied per $100 of payroll. The more you pay your employees, the higher your premium — but that also reflects more exposure for the carrier.

**Experience modification rate (EMR or mod).** This is the single biggest variable most employers can influence. Your mod compares your actual claims history to the expected claims for businesses of your size and type. A mod of 1.0 is average. Below 1.0 means fewer claims than expected, which lowers your premium. Above 1.0 means more claims than expected, which raises it.

A business with a 0.80 mod pays 20 percent less than the base rate. A business with a 1.30 mod pays 30 percent more. Over time, a bad mod can cost you tens of thousands of dollars in excess premium — and it can also cost you contracts, since many general contractors will not hire subcontractors with high mods.

## The claims process: what happens when an employee gets hurt

When a workplace injury occurs, there is a process that needs to happen quickly:

1. **Get the employee medical treatment.** This is the first priority, always.
2. **Report the injury to your insurance carrier.** Do this within 24 hours if possible. Delayed reporting is one of the top reasons claims spiral out of control.
3. **File the required forms.** Kentucky requires employers to file a First Report of Injury with the Kentucky Department of Workers' Claims. Your carrier will typically handle this, but it is your responsibility to make sure it gets done.
4. **Cooperate with the claims adjuster.** The carrier will assign an adjuster to manage the claim. Answer their questions, provide documentation, and stay engaged with the process.
5. **Facilitate return to work.** When the employee is cleared for any level of work, getting them back on modified or light duty reduces claim costs and helps the employee recover.

The Kentucky Department of Workers' Claims oversees the entire system and resolves disputes between employees, employers, and carriers. If a claim is contested, it goes through their administrative process rather than the regular court system.

## Tips for managing workers comp costs

Workers comp does not have to be a runaway expense. The employers who pay the least relative to their size are the ones who manage the process proactively.

- **Invest in safety.** A documented safety program with regular training reduces injuries and can qualify you for premium credits. This is the single highest-return investment most small businesses can make in their insurance costs.
- **Classify employees correctly.** If you have office staff coded as field workers, you are overpaying. Review your classification codes every year, especially if job roles have changed.
- **Report claims immediately.** Late reporting is the enemy of good outcomes. The faster a claim is reported, the faster treatment begins, and the lower the overall cost tends to be.
- **Use light-duty programs.** Keeping an injured worker engaged, even in a limited capacity, reduces lost-time costs and keeps your mod from climbing.
- **Work with an independent agent.** Workers comp carriers vary significantly in their appetite for different industries, and an independent agent can shop the market for you. Pair your workers comp with a strong [general liability policy](/commercial/general-liability.html) for a complete risk management foundation.
- **Review your mod worksheet.** Your experience mod is calculated from data, and data can contain errors. Have your agent review the worksheet annually to make sure closed claims are reflected correctly and no mistakes are inflating your mod.

## Common questions about Kentucky workers comp

**Do I need workers comp if I only have part-time employees?**
Yes. Kentucky's requirement applies to employers with one or more employees regardless of hours worked.

**Do subcontractors need workers' comp in Kentucky?**
Yes, once the subcontractor has one employee ([KRS 342.630](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=32533)). If a subcontractor hasn't secured coverage, the contractor that hired it and that contractor's insurer are liable for the subcontractor's injured employees, and can recover the cost from the sub ([KRS 342.610(2)](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=47623)).

**Do sole proprietors need workers' comp in Kentucky?**
A sole proprietor with no employees is not required to carry it. Owners are covered only if they elect coverage by an endorsement on a workers' comp policy ([KRS 342.012](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=44523)). Once you hire an employee, the business must cover that employee ([KRS 342.630](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=32533)).

**Can I use a PEO or employee leasing company instead of buying my own policy?**
You can, and many small businesses do. The PEO carries the coverage and you pay them a fee that includes the workers comp cost. Just make sure you understand what happens if you leave the PEO arrangement — you will need your own policy immediately.

**What if my employee was at fault for the injury?**
Workers comp is a no-fault system. Benefits are paid regardless of who caused the injury, with very limited exceptions for intoxication or intentional self-harm.

**Does workers comp cover employees who work remotely?**
Generally yes, if the injury arises out of and in the course of employment. Remote work injuries can be more complicated to document, so clear remote work policies help.
