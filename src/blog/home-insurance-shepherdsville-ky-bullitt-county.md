Home insurance in Shepherdsville, KY is priced by your own house, your roof, your coverage choices and one local fact that surprises many homeowners: a large share of the city sits in a floodplain. Kentucky's statewide average is high, so it pays to look closely at what you are buying before your next renewal.

This guide covers what affects a Shepherdsville homeowners policy, what a standard policy leaves out, and how to compare options without guessing.

## How much is home insurance in Shepherdsville, KY?

There is no single Shepherdsville price, and we will not quote you one from a generic rate table. Local averages on quote-aggregator sites are often dated and built from a handful of sample homes, so treat them with caution.

What we can point to is the statewide picture. Insurance.com's 2026 rate analysis puts the Kentucky average at $4,471 a year for a home with $300,000 of dwelling coverage, $300,000 of liability coverage and a $1,000 deductible, compared with a national average of $2,872 ([Insurance.com](https://www.insurance.com/home-and-renters-insurance/home-insurance-basics/average-homeowners-insurance-rates-by-state)). Your premium may land well above or below that, depending on the factors below.

For a deeper look at why Kentucky's numbers keep climbing, see our guide to [why Kentucky home insurance rates are rising](/blog/kentucky-home-insurance-rates-rising-2026). This post stays focused on Shepherdsville and the rest of Bullitt County.

## What affects a Bullitt County home insurance rate?

Your ZIP code is one input, but it is rarely the biggest. These usually matter more:

- **Rebuilding cost.** The cost to rebuild your home, not its sale price, sets how much dwelling coverage you need.
- **Roof age and condition.** Older roofs often cost more to insure and may be paid at depreciated value after a storm.
- **Deductibles.** A higher deductible lowers the premium, but only if you could pay it tomorrow.
- **Claims history.** Past claims on the home and on you both count.
- **Bundling.** Combining home and auto can help, and [here is how much bundling really saves](/blog/bundling-home-auto-insurance-savings).

The Insurance Information Institute advises that you should not confuse what you paid for your house with rebuilding costs, and that the land under it is not at risk from covered perils, so its value does not belong in your coverage limit ([Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/12-ways-to-lower-your-homeowners-insurance-costs)). That matters in a market where purchase prices can run ahead of or behind construction costs.

## Is Shepherdsville in a flood zone?

Part of it is, and the Salt River is the reason. The regional hazard mitigation page from the KIPDA planning agency says 37.6 percent of Shepherdsville is in a floodplain, the highest share of any city in its region, and 17.4 percent of Bullitt County is, the highest of any county there ([KIPDA](https://www.kipda.org/community-economic-development/regional-hazard-mitigation/hazards/flooding/)). The same page describes the city as particularly vulnerable because the Salt River flows through it.

The river is not a hypothetical. In April 2025, Spectrum News reported residents evacuating homes along the Salt River in Shepherdsville ([Spectrum News 1](https://spectrumnews1.com/ky/louisville/news/2025/04/06/evacuations--national-guard-deployed-in-shepherdsville)), and the river later reached nearly five feet above flood stage in the city ([Spectrum News 1](https://spectrumnews1.com/ky/louisville/news/2025/04/10/salt-river-recedes--residents-muck-out)). KIPDA also records earlier crests, including 35.01 feet in February 2018 and about 33.1 feet in April 2011, against a 32-foot flood stage.

> **Key takeaway:** Standard homeowners insurance does not pay for flood damage. FEMA says most homeowners policies do not cover flood, and that a National Flood Insurance Program policy typically has a 30-day waiting period before it takes effect ([FEMA](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance)).

That waiting period is the practical lesson. A flood policy bought when the river is already rising generally will not help with that storm. If your mortgage lender requires flood insurance, ask us about a flood quote alongside your home policy. If it does not, ask whether you should carry it anyway, especially close to the river. ## What does growth in Bullitt County mean for new homeowners?

Bullitt County keeps adding neighbors. Census Bureau estimates put the county at 86,454 residents on July 1, 2025, up from 82,224 in the April 2020 base ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/popest/tables/2020-2025/counties/totals/co-est2025-pop-21.xlsx)). Many of those households are new to the I-65 corridor and setting up insurance for the first time.

If you are buying new construction or a recently built home, three questions are worth asking:

- **Was the dwelling limit built from a rebuilding estimate?** A sale price that includes the lot, builder margin or upgrades is not the same number.
- **Does the policy pay roof damage at replacement cost or actual cash value?** Our guide to [replacement cost vs. actual cash value](/blog/replacement-cost-vs-actual-cash-value) shows how the difference plays out in a claim.
- **Is there a separate wind and hail deductible?** Many policies use a percentage rather than a flat dollar amount.

## What is a wind and hail deductible?

It is a separate deductible that applies only to wind or hail damage. Instead of a flat $1,000, it can be a share of your home's insured value. United Policyholders says wind deductibles usually run 2 to 10 percent of your home's insured value ([United Policyholders](https://uphelp.org/claim-guidance-publications/whats-up-with-home-insurance-deductibles/)).

Here is what that looks like. On a $300,000 dwelling limit, a 2 percent deductible means the first $6,000 of a covered hail claim is yours. Read your declarations page for the exact figure, and decide ahead of time whether you could cover it.

## What if no company will insure my home?

Talk to an agent about the Kentucky FAIR Plan. It offers Dwelling Fire, Commercial Fire, Commercial Farm Fire and Homeowners coverage for property owners who cannot get coverage elsewhere, and you apply through any licensed Kentucky producer ([Kentucky Insurance Plans](https://kyinsplans.org/fair/consumers/)). It is a last resort, so compare standard carriers first.
