Your insurance policy might be 40 pages of dense legal language, but the declarations page is the one page you actually need to read. It is the summary of everything that matters: what is covered, how much you pay, and what your limits are.

Most people file their policy away and never look at it until something goes wrong. That is a mistake. Your declarations page, sometimes called the "dec page," takes five minutes to read and can save you from finding out the hard way that your coverage has changed.

## What is a declarations page?

The declarations page is the first page (or first few pages) of your insurance policy. It summarizes the key details of your coverage in plain terms. Think of it as the table of contents for your policy, except it also contains all the numbers that matter.

Every type of insurance has a declarations page: [homeowners](/personal/home.html), [auto](/personal/auto.html), commercial, umbrella, and more. The format varies by carrier, but the information is largely the same.

Your carrier sends you an updated dec page every time you renew or make a change to your policy. You should read it every time.

## The sections of a declarations page

Here is what you will find on a typical declarations page and what each section means.

### Named insured

This is who the policy covers. On a homeowners policy, this should include everyone who owns the home. On an auto policy, it should list all drivers in the household.

Check this section to make sure:

- Your name is spelled correctly
- All relevant people are listed (a new spouse, for example)
- The mailing address is current

An incorrect named insured can cause problems during a claim. If you have gotten married, divorced, or added someone to your mortgage, make sure the dec page reflects that.

### Policy number and policy period

Your policy number is your unique identifier with the carrier. The policy period shows the start and end dates of your coverage. Most policies run for six months (auto) or twelve months (homeowners).

If your policy has lapsed or there is a gap in coverage, it will show up here. Make sure the dates are continuous from one term to the next.

### Property or vehicle information

For homeowners, this section lists the property address, the year the home was built, the construction type, and sometimes the square footage.

For auto, it lists each vehicle by year, make, model, and VIN.

Check this section to make sure the information is accurate. An incorrect year built or wrong VIN can affect your premium and your coverage.

### Coverage limits

This is the most important section. It lists every coverage on your policy along with the dollar limit for each.

On a homeowners dec page, you will typically see:

- **Dwelling coverage (Coverage A):** The amount the insurer will pay to rebuild your home. This should reflect the current replacement cost, not the market value.
- **Other structures (Coverage B):** Covers detached structures like garages, fences, and sheds. Usually set at 10% of your dwelling coverage.
- **Personal property (Coverage C):** Covers your belongings. Usually 50-75% of your dwelling coverage.
- **Loss of use (Coverage D):** Covers living expenses if your home becomes uninhabitable. Typically 20-30% of dwelling coverage.
- **Personal liability (Coverage E):** Covers legal and medical costs if someone is injured on your property or you cause damage to someone else's property.
- **Medical payments (Coverage F):** Covers minor medical expenses for guests injured on your property, regardless of fault.

On an auto dec page, you will see liability limits (often shown as three numbers like 100/300/100), collision, comprehensive, uninsured motorist, and PIP if you are in Kentucky.

### Deductibles

Your deductible is what you pay out of pocket before insurance kicks in. The dec page lists your deductible for each coverage.

Watch for these details:

- **Flat deductible vs. percentage deductible.** A flat deductible is a fixed dollar amount ($1,000, $2,500). A percentage deductible is a percentage of your dwelling coverage. A 2% deductible on a $300,000 home means you pay $6,000 before insurance pays anything.
- **Separate wind/hail deductible.** Some policies in storm-prone areas have a separate, higher deductible for wind and hail damage. This is common in Kentucky and can catch people off guard during storm season.
- **All-peril vs. named-peril.** Your deductible may differ depending on the type of damage.

Make sure your deductible is an amount you can actually afford to pay. A higher deductible lowers your premium, but only helps if you can cover it when you need to.

### Endorsements and riders

Endorsements are add-ons that modify your base policy. They might add coverage (like water backup coverage or scheduled jewelry) or restrict it (like an animal liability exclusion).

Your dec page lists all active endorsements. Read through them. Common endorsements include:

- Water backup and sump overflow
- Scheduled personal property (jewelry, firearms, art)
- Home business coverage
- Identity theft protection
- Equipment breakdown

If you see an endorsement you do not recognize, ask your agent what it does and whether you still need it.

### Premium breakdown

The dec page shows your total premium and usually breaks it down by coverage. This is useful for understanding where your money goes.

If your premium went up at renewal, compare the old and new dec pages line by line. Sometimes the increase is across the board. Sometimes one specific coverage jumped, which might mean a change in risk assessment or a rate filing by the carrier.

## What to check every renewal

When your renewal dec page arrives, do not just file it away. Compare it to your previous one and check for:

- **Coverage limit changes.** Did your dwelling coverage increase with inflation? Did any limits decrease?
- **Deductible changes.** Did a new wind/hail deductible appear?
- **New endorsements.** Were any added or removed?
- **Premium changes.** If your premium went up, which coverages drove the increase?
- **Accurate information.** Is your address, vehicle information, or named insured still correct?

If anything looks wrong or unexpected, call your agent before the new term starts.

## Common mistakes people make with their dec page

**Never reading it.** The majority of policyholders never look at their declarations page. They find out what their coverage actually says only after they file a claim.

**Assuming dwelling coverage equals market value.** Dwelling coverage should reflect what it costs to rebuild your home, not what you could sell it for. These numbers can be very different, especially in Kentucky where land values and construction costs do not always move together.

**Ignoring endorsement changes.** Carriers sometimes add restrictive endorsements at renewal. A new animal liability exclusion, for example, could leave you exposed if your dog bites someone. Read the endorsement list every time.

**Not updating after life changes.** New purchases, renovations, a new vehicle, or a change in household members all require policy updates. If your dec page does not reflect your current situation, you may have coverage gaps.

## Keep your dec pages organized

Save every declarations page you receive, either digitally or in a folder. Having prior dec pages makes it easier to spot changes, dispute billing errors, and provide proof of coverage to lenders or landlords.

If you cannot find your current dec page, call your agent or log into your carrier's website. Most carriers make your current declarations page available online.

### FAQ: What is a declarations page in insurance?

A declarations page (or "dec page") is the summary page of your insurance policy. It lists your coverage limits, deductibles, premium, policy period, named insureds, and endorsements. It is typically the first page of your policy and the most important one to review.

### FAQ: How often should I review my declarations page?

Review your declarations page every time you receive a renewal notice and after any policy change. At minimum, review it once a year. Compare it to your previous dec page to catch any changes in coverage, deductibles, or endorsements.

### FAQ: What should I do if something on my declarations page is wrong?

Contact your insurance agent immediately. Incorrect information on your dec page, such as a wrong address, missing driver, or inaccurate home details, can affect your coverage and your ability to file a claim. Corrections should be made before the policy period begins if possible.

### FAQ: Is the declarations page the same as my full policy?

No. The declarations page is a summary. Your full policy includes the declarations page plus the policy jacket (the detailed terms, conditions, and exclusions). The dec page tells you what and how much is covered. The full policy explains when and how coverage applies.
