The end of the year is a natural time to take stock. You review your finances, set goals, and plan for the year ahead. Your insurance should be part of that process.

Life changes throughout the year — a new car, a home renovation, a teenager who started driving, a new baby, a side business, a change in income. Every one of these affects your insurance needs. If you do not review your policies at least once a year, your coverage drifts out of alignment with your actual life, and gaps appear that you will not notice until you file a claim.

Here is a practical checklist to walk through before the calendar turns.

## Home insurance review

Pull out your homeowners [declarations page](/blog/how-to-read-declarations-page.html) and check these items:

**Is your dwelling coverage still accurate?** Construction costs have risen significantly in recent years. If your policy insures your home for $200,000 but it would cost $280,000 to rebuild today, you are underinsured. Ask your agent to run a replacement cost estimate.

**Did you make home improvements this year?** A new roof, a kitchen renovation, a finished basement, a deck, an addition — all of these increase your home's replacement value and should be reflected in your coverage.

**Are your personal property limits adequate?** Think about major purchases you made this year. New furniture, electronics, appliances, or other items increase the total value of your belongings. If you have outgrown your current limits, adjust them.

**Do you have the right endorsements?** Check whether you have sewer backup coverage, water damage coverage, and scheduled items for high-value belongings like jewelry, art, or firearms. If you acquired any high-value items this year, schedule them on your policy.

**Has your risk profile changed?** Did you install a trampoline, get a dog, add a swimming pool, or start a home-based business? These all affect your homeowners policy — either your coverage or your premium, and sometimes both.

## Auto insurance review

**Did you add or remove a vehicle?** Make sure every vehicle you own is listed on your policy with the correct coverage levels.

**Did a teenager start driving?** Adding a young driver to your policy is one of the biggest premium increases you will experience. Make sure they are properly listed and that you have adequate liability limits.

**Has your commute changed?** If you changed jobs, started working from home, or moved, your annual mileage may have changed. Lower mileage can reduce your premium.

**Are you carrying the right coverage for each vehicle?** If you have a vehicle that is paid off and worth less than $5,000, you might consider dropping collision and comprehensive to save on premiums. But if you could not afford to replace it, keep the coverage.

**Is your liability coverage high enough?** Most agents recommend at least 100/300/100. If you have been carrying state minimums, now is a good time to increase.

**Do you have uninsured motorist coverage?** Kentucky's uninsured driver rate is around 12 percent. If you dropped or reduced UM/UIM coverage, reconsider.

## Life insurance review

**Has your family situation changed?** Marriage, divorce, a new child, or a death in the family all affect your life insurance needs. If you had a baby this year, your coverage needs likely went up.

**Is your beneficiary designation current?** Check the named beneficiary on every life insurance policy. After a marriage, divorce, or death, beneficiary designations can become outdated. This is one of the most commonly overlooked items and one of the most important.

**Is your coverage amount still adequate?** A general guideline is 10 to 15 times your annual income. If your income increased this year, your coverage may need to increase with it.

**Is your term policy approaching expiration?** If you have term life insurance and the end of the term is within the next few years, start planning now. Renewal rates at the end of a term are usually much higher. You may want to convert to permanent coverage or shop for a new term policy while your health is still favorable.

## Health and Medicare review

**Did your employer change health plans?** Open enrollment for employer-sponsored plans typically happens in November and December. Review your options and compare premiums, deductibles, copays, and provider networks.

**Are you approaching 65?** Medicare eligibility begins at 65. Your Initial Enrollment Period starts three months before your birth month. If you are turning 65 in 2027, start planning now.

**Did your prescriptions change?** If your doctor added or changed medications this year, make sure your current plan covers them at a reasonable cost. This is especially important for Medicare Part D plans.

## Business insurance review (if applicable)

**Did your revenue change?** Many commercial insurance policies are rated based on revenue. If your business grew (or contracted) significantly, your coverage limits and premiums should reflect that.

**Did you hire employees?** Adding employees affects your workers' compensation, general liability, and potentially your health insurance obligations.

**Did you add equipment or vehicles?** New business assets need to be covered under your commercial property or commercial auto policy.

**Do you have the right coverage for new services?** If your business expanded into new services, products, or markets this year, your existing coverage may not apply. A policy that covered you as a web designer may not cover you if you started offering IT consulting.

## Umbrella policy review

**Do you have an umbrella policy?** If you have assets to protect — a home, savings, retirement accounts — an umbrella policy adds $1 million or more in liability coverage above your auto and home policies for typically $200 to $400 per year. If you do not have one, this is worth adding.

**Are your underlying limits high enough?** Umbrella policies require minimum underlying limits on your auto and home policies. Make sure your base policies meet those requirements.

## Discounts you might be missing

While reviewing your policies, ask about discounts you may not be receiving:

- **Bundling discounts** for home and auto with the same carrier
- **Claims-free discounts** if you have not filed a claim in three or more years
- **Safety device discounts** for security systems, smoke detectors, or smart home devices
- **Loyalty discounts** for long-term policyholders
- **Good student discounts** if you have a teenager with a 3.0 GPA or higher
- **Low mileage discounts** if you drive less than the average annual mileage
- **Professional or alumni association discounts** through group programs

### FAQ: How often should I review my insurance coverage?

At least once a year, and any time you experience a major life change — buying a home, having a baby, getting married or divorced, changing jobs, starting a business, or making a major purchase. An annual review catches changes you may have forgotten to report throughout the year.

### FAQ: What is the most commonly overlooked item in an insurance review?

Beneficiary designations on life insurance policies. After a marriage, divorce, or death in the family, beneficiary designations often do not get updated. If your ex-spouse is still named as your life insurance beneficiary, the proceeds will go to them regardless of your current wishes.

### FAQ: Can I save money by reviewing my insurance without switching carriers?

Yes. Often the savings come from adjusting coverage levels, raising deductibles, removing coverage you no longer need, or adding discounts you are eligible for. An annual review can uncover savings without requiring you to switch companies.

Bring your current [declarations pages](/blog/how-to-read-declarations-page.html) for all policies (home, auto, umbrella, life), a list of any changes from the past year (new vehicles, renovations, life events), and any questions about your coverage. If you work with an independent agent, they can pull much of this information directly.
