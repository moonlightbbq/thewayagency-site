If your homeowners renewal came in higher than last year, you have plenty of company. In a March 2026 Pew Research Center survey, 71 percent of U.S. homeowners said their insurance costs had gone up over the last few years, and 42 percent said they had gone up a lot ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/05/06/71-of-u-s-homeowners-say-their-home-insurance-costs-have-gone-up/)). Kentucky's premiums are among the highest in the country, and they rose faster than most states' this year.

This guide explains what is driving Kentucky's increases and the practical steps worth taking before your next renewal.

## The short answer

Kentucky home insurance rates are rising mainly because storm claims and the cost to repair and rebuild homes have both gone up, and insurers are pricing older roofs and other features of each home more closely. An Insurance.com analysis published in September 2026 found Kentucky's average premium rose 10.6 percent from 2025 to 2026, to $4,471 a year, the eighth-highest state average in the country ([Insurance.com analysis via Stacker](https://www.boston25news.com/news/states-where-home/SBMRGI3APEZ3DKUMEUYNFIQIDQ/)). You cannot change the weather, but you can make sure your coverage amount, deductibles and discounts fit your home, and compare carriers when a renewal jumps.

## How much Kentucky rates have gone up

Rate studies price different sample homes, so their averages differ. Two recent ones:

- **Insurance.com (2026 rates from Quadrant Information Services):** $4,471 a year for a home with $300,000 of dwelling coverage, $300,000 of liability coverage and a $1,000 deductible, 56 percent above the national average of $2,872 ([Insurance.com](https://www.insurance.com/home-and-renters-insurance/home-insurance-basics/average-homeowners-insurance-rates-by-state)). Its September analysis named Kentucky one of only four states in the top 10 for both the highest premiums and the fastest-rising premiums.
- **LendingTree (quotes from February 2026):** $3,495 a year for $400,000 of dwelling coverage. LendingTree found Kentucky rates rose 10.3 percent from 2024 to 2025, nearly double the 5.6 percent national increase, and about 50 percent from 2021 to 2025, close to the 48 percent national increase over those years ([LendingTree](https://www.lendingtree.com/home-insurance/homeowners-insurance-kentucky/)).

Your own premium will likely differ from both averages. Your home, coverage options, claims history, ZIP code and credit all affect the price, as LendingTree notes.

Nationally, the pressure has not let up. In late 2025, real estate analytics firm Cotality projected that the average homeowners premium would rise 8 percent in 2026 and another 8 percent in 2027, and said insurance now makes up 9 percent of the typical U.S. homeowner's housing payment, the highest average on record ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/real-estate/homeowners-insurance-costs-could-spike-over-next-2-years)).

## Why rates are rising in Kentucky

### Storms keep producing claims

Nationally, wind and hail accounted for the largest share of homeowners claims from 2018 to 2022, according to the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/fact-statistic/facts-statistics-homeowners-and-renters-insurance). Kentucky's recent storm seasons have added to that. The National Weather Service office in Paducah, whose four-state forecast area includes Daviess County, counted 61 tornadoes in 2025, second only to the 79 it recorded in 2011, and its April 2, 2025 outbreak of 23 tornadoes was the largest single event in the office's history ([NWS Paducah](https://www.weather.gov/pah/2025YearInReview)). The Louisville office, which covers Bullitt County, reported ten tornadoes in its region on April 2, 2024 alone ([NWS Louisville](https://www.weather.gov/media/lmk/climate/summaryPDFs/2024_Annual.pdf)). When claim costs climb across a region, rates tend to follow.

### Repairs and rebuilding cost more

Higher costs for labor and building materials make it more expensive to repair and rebuild a home after damage, as Pew's report notes. Realtor.com's chief economist told Fox Business that the higher cost of rebuilding, a reflection of overall inflation and housing supply-chain trends, is pushing premiums higher. A Henderson independent agent interviewed by [Spectrum News 1](https://spectrumnews1.com/ky/louisville/news/2026/05/14/home-insurance-costs) in May 2026 pointed to the same pressures, plus rising prices for reinsurance, the coverage insurers buy for their own large losses.

### Roofs and each home's details get more scrutiny

The same agent said insurers increasingly weigh roof condition, the home's exterior and credit scores, and that older roofs often trigger surcharges. Some carriers now add an endorsement that pays wind and hail damage to the roof surface at actual cash value, the roof's depreciated value, mainly on new policies when the roof is too old to qualify for replacement cost ([Rough Notes](https://roughnotes.com/homeowners-roof-surfacing-coverage-considerations/)). On an older roof, an actual cash value payment may not cover the cost of a new one. Our guide to [replacement cost vs. actual cash value](/blog/replacement-cost-vs-actual-cash-value) walks through an example.

## Who oversees Kentucky home insurance rates

Under Kentucky law, most insurers licensed in the state must file the rates they charge for homeowners and other personal policies with the Commissioner of Insurance ([KRS 304.13-051](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=17055)). The [Kentucky Department of Insurance](https://insurance.ky.gov/) regulates the state's insurance market and takes consumer complaints. Its toll-free number is 800-595-6053.

## What you can do before your next renewal

- **Insure for rebuilding cost, not market value.** The dwelling coverage you need depends on what it would cost to rebuild your home, not its market value (LendingTree), and the land under it is not at risk from fire or wind, so its value does not belong in the limit ([Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/12-ways-to-lower-your-homeowners-insurance-costs)). If you bought a newly built home in Shepherdsville or Mt. Washington, make sure the limit came from a rebuilding estimate rather than the sale price, and revisit it after an addition or major renovation.
- **Know your deductibles, including wind and hail.** Your policy may carry a separate wind and hail deductible, which can be a percentage of your home's insured value instead of a flat dollar amount ([United Policyholders](https://uphelp.org/claim-guidance-publications/whats-up-with-home-insurance-deductibles/)). On a $300,000 dwelling limit, a 2 percent deductible means the first $6,000 of a hail claim is yours. A higher deductible lowers your premium ([NAIC](https://content.naic.org/consumer/homeowners-insurance.htm)), but choose one you could pay tomorrow.
- **Ask how your roof is covered.** Find out whether roof damage is paid at replacement cost or actual cash value, and whether a newer roof would change your price.
- **Use the discounts you qualify for.** Some companies take 5 to 15 percent off when you buy two or more policies from them, though the Insurance Information Institute says to confirm the combined price is actually lower. Many insurers also discount for burglar alarms and deadbolts (NAIC). Here is [how much bundling home and auto really saves](/blog/bundling-home-auto-insurance-savings).
- **Check your credit report.** Kentucky does not prohibit insurers from using credit information to underwrite or price policies, according to the Department of Insurance. State law does bar an insurer from declining, canceling or nonrenewing a personal policy solely because of your credit history, a lack of credit history, or certain life events that hurt your credit, such as a serious illness, the death of a spouse, child or parent, or a divorce ([KRS 304.20-042](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=40553)). The Department suggests correcting errors on your credit report and asking your insurer to re-evaluate you once your credit improves ([Kentucky Department of Insurance](https://insurance.ky.gov/ppc/Documents/KDOI%20CreditScoring060922mn.pdf)).
- **Think before filing a small claim.** Claims history affects your price (LendingTree), and a home's insurance claim history is recorded in a CLUE (Comprehensive Loss Underwriting Exchange) report (Insurance Information Institute). For damage close to your deductible, ask your agent how a claim could affect your renewal before you decide. For serious damage, file the claim: that is what the policy is for.
- **Compare carriers when a renewal jumps.** The same home can be priced very differently by different companies. In LendingTree's Kentucky data, the lowest-priced company it studied averaged about 40 percent below the state average.

## A stronger roof under Kentucky's FORTIFIED law

Kentucky's Strengthen Kentucky Homes Act ties two insurance rules to the FORTIFIED Home roof standards published by the Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety. Both apply to policies issued or renewed on or after March 1, 2026:

- **An optional roof-upgrade rider.** Companies insuring a single-family home whose roof does not meet the FORTIFIED standard must offer an optional rider, endorsement or policy provision. When a covered claim requires replacing the roof, it pays the cost to upgrade the roof structure to the FORTIFIED standard ([KRS 304.13-346](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=57338)). Ask whether your policy includes it and what it adds to your premium.
- **A discount for a certified FORTIFIED home.** Companies must give a premium discount or rate reduction on wind and hail coverage for a home certified to the FORTIFIED standard when the discount is actuarially justified and there is credible evidence of cost savings ([KRS 304.13-342](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=54978)). If your roof has a FORTIFIED certificate, ask how your company applies it.

The state's Strengthen Kentucky Homes grant program offered up to $10,000 toward upgrading a roof to the FORTIFIED standard. As of October 1, 2026 it is no longer accepting new Round 1 applications, and applicants already on its waitlist get first priority for future funding rounds ([Strengthen Kentucky Homes](https://skh.ky.gov)).

## If no standard company will insure your home

The [Kentucky FAIR Plan](https://kyinsplans.org/fair/consumers/) provides basic homeowners and dwelling fire coverage for property owners who cannot get insurance in the standard market for reasons beyond their control. Applications go through a licensed Kentucky agent. Because it is meant for homes the regular market will not take, it is worth comparing standard carriers first.

### FAQ: How much does home insurance cost in Kentucky in 2026?

It depends on the home and the study. Insurance.com put the 2026 Kentucky average at $4,471 a year for $300,000 of dwelling coverage, and LendingTree put it at $3,495 a year for $400,000 of dwelling coverage. Your ZIP code, roof, claims history, credit and coverage choices decide your actual price.

### FAQ: Why did my Kentucky home insurance go up so much this year?

The main drivers are storm claims, especially wind and hail, and higher costs to repair and rebuild homes. Insurers are also pricing roof age and condition more closely. A changed deductible, a lost discount or a recent claim can add to the increase, so compare this year's declarations page with last year's.

### FAQ: Does my credit affect my home insurance rate in Kentucky?

It can. Kentucky does not prohibit insurers from using credit information to underwrite or price policies. State law does not allow an insurer to decline, cancel or nonrenew a personal policy solely because of credit history or certain life events, such as a serious illness or a divorce.

### FAQ: What is a wind and hail deductible?

It is a separate deductible that applies only to wind and hail damage. It can be a percentage of your dwelling coverage rather than a flat amount, so a 2 percent deductible on a $300,000 dwelling limit means you pay the first $6,000 of a hail claim.
