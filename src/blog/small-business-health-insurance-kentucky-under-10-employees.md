Health insurance for a small business with less than 10 employees in Kentucky is a choice, not a requirement: the federal employer mandate applies only to employers with an average of at least 50 full-time employees, counting full-time equivalents ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/affordable-care-act/employers/employer-shared-responsibility-provisions)). If you want to offer a benefit anyway, you have three main routes: a small group plan, a QSEHRA, or an ICHRA. One thing has changed recently: the kynect SHOP marketplace no longer enrolls new employers, so a licensed agent is now the practical way to find group coverage.

This guide walks through each option for a team of fewer than 10, and it does not repeat our broader post on [group health insurance for small businesses in Kentucky](/blog/group-health-insurance-small-business-kentucky), which covers plan tiers, pricing factors and setup steps for small groups of any size.

## Do I have to offer health insurance with fewer than 10 employees?

No. The IRS says most employers fall below the 50-employee threshold and are not subject to the employer shared responsibility rules ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/affordable-care-act/employers/employer-shared-responsibility-provisions)). A business with five or eight people has no federal penalty for skipping coverage.

That does not make the decision free. Owners along the I-65 corridor in Bullitt County compete with Louisville employers for the same workers, and Owensboro businesses compete for the same local talent. A modest health benefit can be the difference when a candidate has two offers. The rest of this post is about doing it without a plan that outgrows your budget.

## What happened to kynect SHOP for small businesses?

The Kentucky Health Benefit Exchange says Anthem, the issuer that offered SHOP plans, will no longer offer SHOP health or dental plans through the marketplace, and employers can no longer apply and enroll in SHOP coverage in Kentucky ([Kentucky Health Benefit Exchange](https://khbe.ky.gov/Enrollment/Pages/SHOP.aspx)). Current enrollees see no disruption in coverage, and the change does not affect the individual health and dental plans on kynect.

The exchange tells small businesses to work directly with Anthem or to talk with a licensed agent or broker about other group coverage. That is the main reason this guide leans on options outside the marketplace. Older articles that describe kynect SHOP as open for new employers are out of date.

## Can a group with fewer than 10 employees buy a small group plan?

Yes. Kentucky law defines a small group as a small employer with two to 50 employees ([KRS 304.17A-005](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/LAW/STATUTES/statute.aspx?id=54530)), so a team of nine fits easily. The two-person minimum is the part that matters most to very small employers: a business with only an owner generally has no group to insure and looks at individual coverage instead.

An independent agent can request quotes from the carriers that sell small group plans in your area and put them side by side. Our [group health page](/health/group-health.html) explains how that process works. For participation rules, rating factors and the setup checklist, use the broader post linked above.

With fewer than 10 people, one or two employees who decline or already have other coverage can change whether a plan works for you. Ask early how the carrier counts eligible employees and who may be waived.

## What is a QSEHRA and how is it different from a group plan?

A QSEHRA (Qualified Small Employer Health Reimbursement Arrangement) is not insurance. You set a monthly allowance, employees buy their own individual coverage, and you reimburse them tax-free. HealthCare.gov describes it as an option for employers with generally fewer than 50 full-time employees that do not offer a group health plan ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/small-businesses/learn-more/qsehra/)).

Key rules for 2026:

- **The cap.** For taxable years beginning in 2026, a QSEHRA cannot reimburse more than $6,450 a year for self-only coverage, or $13,100 when family members are covered ([IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf), section 4.63).
- **Same terms for everyone.** It must be offered on the same terms to all full-time employees. The amount may vary only by an employee's age and the number of people covered ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/small-businesses/learn-more/qsehra/)).
- **No group plan alongside it.** You cannot offer a group health plan, including SHOP coverage or a flexible spending account, to any employees ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/small-businesses/learn-more/qsehra/)).
- **Employees need coverage.** Employees must have qualifying health coverage, such as a marketplace plan, Medicare or Medicaid, before they can use the funds.
- **Written notice.** You owe new employees a written notice when they become eligible and current employees one at least 90 days before the plan year begins.

The appeal for a small employer is a fixed, predictable cost. You pick the allowance, and a rate increase from a carrier does not change what you pay.

## What is an ICHRA and who is it for?

An ICHRA (Individual Coverage Health Reimbursement Arrangement) works the same way in spirit: you reimburse employees for individual health coverage. The differences are in who can offer it and how it is structured. A CMS overview says employers of all sizes may offer an ICHRA, and that employers offering one generally cannot also offer traditional group coverage to employees in the same class ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/marketplace/technical-assistance-resources/health-reimbursement-arrangements-overview.pdf)).

A few points from that overview matter for a small team:

- An ICHRA is a group health plan funded only by employer contributions, and reimbursements are excluded from the employee's income and payroll taxes.
- Employees generally get a written notice at least 90 days before the plan year begins. New hires get theirs no later than the date coverage can begin.
- An employee offered an affordable ICHRA cannot receive a premium tax credit for marketplace coverage, so the affordability math matters for lower-paid staff.

The same overview is dated 2020, so ask us or your benefits attorney to confirm current details before you commit. For a business with fewer than 50 employees, the QSEHRA is often the simpler of the two. The ICHRA fits better if you expect to grow past 50 or want to set different allowances for different classes of employees.

## How does the Small Business Health Care Tax Credit work for a team under 10?

The credit can be worth up to 50 percent of the premiums a small business pays, or up to 35 percent for a small tax-exempt employer ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/affordable-care-act/employers/small-business-health-care-tax-credit-and-the-shop-marketplace)). To qualify, you generally need:

- **Fewer than 25 full-time equivalent employees** ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/affordable-care-act/employers/small-business-health-care-tax-credit-and-the-shop-marketplace)). Owners, their family members and seasonal workers who work 120 or fewer days are not counted in that total ([Form 8941 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8941)).
- **Average annual wages below an inflation-adjusted limit.** For taxable years beginning in 2026, the base dollar amount in the credit's formula is $34,100 ([IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf), section 3.09).
- **An employer contribution of at least half.** You must pay a uniform percentage, not less than 50 percent, of the premium cost ([Form 8941 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8941)).

The credit is also a sliding scale. It shrinks once you have more than 10 FTEs, and it shrinks again as average wages rise above that dollar amount ([Form 8941 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8941)). That is why a team of fewer than 10 with modest average wages is closest to the full credit. It is available for two consecutive tax years.

Here is the catch for Kentucky. The credit requires that you pay premiums through a SHOP marketplace, or qualify for an exception. The 2025 Form 8941 instructions list exceptions for Hawaii and for certain counties without SHOP plans, and name no Kentucky exception ([Form 8941 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8941)). The state says tax credits may be available to businesses that had SHOP coverage in tax year 2025 or 2026 ([Kentucky Health Benefit Exchange](https://khbe.ky.gov/Enrollment/Pages/SHOP.aspx)). If you are counting on this credit, talk to your tax preparer before you choose a plan.

## Which option fits a business with fewer than 10 employees?

There is no single right answer, but some patterns help:

- **You want a traditional group plan** and have at least two eligible employees: ask an agent to quote small group plans.
- **You want a fixed budget** and are comfortable with employees shopping for their own plans: a QSEHRA is built for that.
- **You may grow past 50 employees or want different allowances by employee class:** look at an ICHRA.
- **You are a sole owner with no employees:** group coverage generally does not apply, and individual coverage is the route.

Whatever you choose, keep health coverage separate in your mind from [workers' compensation](/commercial/workers-compensation.html). Workers' comp covers work-related injuries, while health coverage handles everything else.

### FAQ: Does a small business with fewer than 10 employees have to offer health insurance in Kentucky?

Not under the federal employer mandate, which applies to employers with an average of at least 50 full-time employees including full-time equivalents ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/affordable-care-act/employers/employer-shared-responsibility-provisions)). Offering coverage is your choice.

### FAQ: Can I still use kynect SHOP for my small business in Kentucky?

Not to start new coverage. The Kentucky Health Benefit Exchange says employers can no longer apply and enroll in SHOP coverage in Kentucky ([Kentucky Health Benefit Exchange](https://khbe.ky.gov/Enrollment/Pages/SHOP.aspx)). It points small businesses to Anthem directly or to a licensed agent or broker.

### FAQ: What is the 2026 QSEHRA limit?

For taxable years beginning in 2026, a QSEHRA can reimburse up to $6,450 a year for self-only coverage and $13,100 for family coverage ([IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf)).

### FAQ: How many employees do you need for small group coverage in Kentucky?

Kentucky defines a small group as a small employer with two to 50 employees ([KRS 304.17A-005](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/LAW/STATUTES/statute.aspx?id=54530)). A business with only an owner generally has no group to insure.

### FAQ: Can a QSEHRA and a group health plan be offered together?

No. A QSEHRA is for employers that do not offer a group health plan to any employees ([HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/small-businesses/learn-more/qsehra/)). An ICHRA generally cannot be paired with traditional group coverage for the same class of employees ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/marketplace/technical-assistance-resources/health-reimbursement-arrangements-overview.pdf)).

### FAQ: Does the Small Business Health Care Tax Credit apply if I use a QSEHRA?

The credit is tied to premiums paid for employee coverage through a SHOP marketplace, or under an IRS exception ([Form 8941 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8941)). A QSEHRA reimburses employees for individual coverage rather than paying a SHOP premium, so ask your tax preparer before assuming it counts.
