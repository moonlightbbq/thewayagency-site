Individual health insurance is a policy you purchase on your own rather than receiving through an employer. In Kentucky, you can buy individual plans through the state marketplace, kynect (kynect.ky.gov), or directly from insurance carriers. Marketplace plans may qualify for premium tax credits and cost-sharing reductions based on your household income, making comprehensive coverage affordable for many Kentuckians who do not have employer-sponsored insurance.

We're not just selling insurance. We're here to make sure you understand your options, feel confident in your coverage, and have someone in your corner when it matters most.

Who needs individual health in Kentucky?

Individual health insurance is essential for self-employed Kentuckians, freelancers, gig workers, small business owners who cannot form a group, early retirees not yet eligible for Medicare, and anyone between jobs. In western Kentucky, where small businesses and self-employment are common, individual plans through kynect are often the primary coverage option. If your household income is between 100% and 400% of the federal poverty level, you likely qualify for premium subsidies; enhanced subsidies extended through recent legislation have made even higher earners eligible for assistance. Open enrollment on kynect runs from November 1 through January 15 annually, and the Way Agency helps clients in the Owensboro area navigate plan selection, subsidy calculations, and enrollment every year.

What does individual health cover?

  • All ACA essential health benefits: hospitalization, prescriptions, maternity, mental health, preventive care
  • Preventive services with no cost-sharing (annual physicals, cancer screenings, immunizations)
  • Emergency and urgent care services
  • Prescription drugs (formulary varies by plan and metal tier)
  • Mental health and substance abuse services
  • Rehabilitative services and devices
  • Lab work and imaging
  • Pediatric dental and vision for children under 19

What individual health does NOT cover

  • Adult dental and vision (available as separate standalone plans)
  • Cosmetic surgery
  • Long-term care or custodial nursing facility care
  • Care outside the plan's provider network (HMO plans) or at higher cost (PPO plans)
  • Experimental treatments not approved by the plan
  • Non-emergency care outside the U.S.

What does individual health cost?

Monthly premiums for individual plans in Kentucky vary widely by metal tier: Bronze plans (lowest premium, highest deductible) start around $250 to $350 per month for a 40-year-old before subsidies, while Gold plans run $450 to $650. With premium tax credits, many Kentuckians pay significantly less. A 40-year-old earning $35,000 might pay $100 to $200 per month after subsidies. Silver plans are often the best value because they unlock cost-sharing reductions for those earning under 250% FPL, lowering deductibles and copays substantially. The Way Agency runs actual subsidy calculations before recommending a metal tier, because the cheapest premium is not always the cheapest total cost of care.

Frequently asked questions

The annual Open Enrollment Period on kynect runs from November 1 through January 15. Outside of open enrollment, you can only enroll during a Special Enrollment Period triggered by a qualifying life event such as losing other coverage, getting married, having a baby, or moving to a new area. Medicaid and KCHIP enrollment through kynect is available year-round.

Premium tax credits are based on your household income relative to the federal poverty level. If your income is between 100% and 400% FPL, you likely qualify, and enhanced subsidies currently extend assistance to higher earners as well. For a single person in 2026, 400% FPL is approximately $60,240. The Way Agency can estimate your subsidy amount before you apply by reviewing your expected annual income.

The metal tiers reflect how costs are shared between you and the insurer. Bronze plans cover about 60% of costs (low premiums, high deductibles). Silver covers 70% and unlocks extra cost-sharing reductions for lower-income enrollees. Gold covers 80% with moderate premiums and lower out-of-pocket costs. Platinum covers 90% with the highest premiums but lowest cost at the point of care. All tiers cover the same essential benefits.
