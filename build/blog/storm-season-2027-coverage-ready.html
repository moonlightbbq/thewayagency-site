"Storm Season 2027: Is Your Kentucky Coverage Ready?"

By The Way Agency, Independent Insurance Agency · March 24, 2027 · 6 min read

Kentucky's severe weather season typically runs from March through June, with the worst tornado and hail activity concentrated in April and May. If you have lived here for any length of time, you know what that means: sirens, warnings, and the occasional reminder that the weather here can turn dangerous without much notice.

You cannot control the weather. But you can control whether your insurance is ready for it. Here is a pre-season checklist for Kentucky homeowners heading into storm season 2027, along with a few things that have changed in the insurance market since last year.

The 2027 pre-season insurance checklist

1. Verify your dwelling coverage amount

This is the single most important number on your homeowners policy. Your dwelling coverage (Coverage A) should reflect what it would cost to rebuild your home today, not what you paid for it and not what it would sell for.

Construction costs have continued to climb, and if your dwelling coverage has not kept pace, you are underinsured. A home that cost $200,000 to build five years ago might cost $260,000 or more to rebuild today. Check your declarations page and compare your Coverage A limit to current local rebuild estimates.

Many carriers offer an inflation guard endorsement that automatically increases your dwelling coverage each year. If you do not have one, ask about it. If you do have one, verify that the automatic increase has kept pace with actual construction costs in your area.

2. Check your deductible structure

Know what you will pay out of pocket before your insurance kicks in. And pay attention to whether you have separate deductibles for different types of claims:

  • Standard deductible: This is the flat dollar amount (often $1,000 to $2,500) that applies to most claims.
  • Wind and hail deductible: Some Kentucky carriers have moved to percentage-based deductibles for wind and hail damage. A 2 percent deductible on a $250,000 home means you pay $5,000 out of pocket on a wind or hail claim. That is significantly more than a flat $1,000 deductible.

If you have a percentage-based wind and hail deductible and were not aware of it, this is the time to find out, not after a storm has already hit.

3. Confirm your roof coverage

How your roof is covered matters more than almost anything else in a storm claim. Roof damage is the most common claim from severe weather, and carriers have tightened their roof coverage in recent years.

Check whether your roof is covered at replacement cost or actual cash value. If your roof is older than 15 to 20 years, some carriers apply an ACV endorsement that depreciates your payout based on the roof's age. The difference between a $20,000 replacement cost payout and a $10,000 ACV payout is the difference between getting a new roof and paying out of pocket.

If your roof has an ACV limitation, discuss your options with your agent. Replacing the roof before a storm hits may actually save you money in the long run by qualifying you for better coverage terms and lower premiums.

4. Review your personal property coverage

Your home's structure is only part of the equation. If a tornado goes through your house, everything inside is affected too. Check your personal property coverage (Coverage C) and make sure the limit is adequate.

More importantly, check whether your personal property is covered at replacement cost or actual cash value. The base policy often defaults to ACV for contents. A replacement cost endorsement on personal property is inexpensive and means your payout reflects what it costs to buy new items, not the depreciated value of your old ones.

5. Confirm you have adequate liability coverage

Storm season is not just about property damage. Fallen trees, damaged fences, and debris from your property can injure neighbors or damage their property. Your liability coverage protects you if someone makes a claim against you for damage originating from your property.

Standard homeowners policies include at least $100,000 in liability, but higher limits are available and recommended. If you have significant assets to protect, consider an umbrella policy for additional liability coverage.

6. Check your flood and water coverage

Standard homeowners policies do not cover flood damage. If your home is in a flood-prone area, particularly along the Ohio River in Owensboro, the Green River, or in low-lying areas near Bowling Green and other Kentucky communities, a separate flood policy is essential.

Also check whether your policy includes water backup coverage. Storm-related sewer backups and sump pump failures cause significant damage, and this coverage is added by endorsement, not included in the base policy.

What has changed in the Kentucky insurance market for 2027

A few trends are worth noting:

Roof coverage restrictions continue. More carriers are applying ACV to older roofs or requiring higher deductibles for wind and hail claims. If your carrier added a roof restriction at your last renewal, this is a trend, not an anomaly. An independent agent can find carriers with more favorable roof terms.

Some carriers have tightened wind and hail deductibles. Percentage-based wind and hail deductibles have become more common in Kentucky, particularly in western Kentucky where storm activity is highest. Read your renewal documents carefully.

Claims from 2026 storms are affecting 2027 rates. Kentucky saw active severe weather in 2026, and the claims from those events are factored into 2027 pricing. If your rate increased at renewal, the broader claims environment is a likely contributor, even if you did not file a claim yourself.

The market is still competitive for well-maintained homes. Despite the challenges, carriers are still competing for good risks. Homes with newer roofs, updated electrical and plumbing, and no recent claims history are attractive to multiple carriers. If you have taken care of your home and have a clean claims record, shopping your coverage can yield real savings.

Before the storm: document everything

Take 20 minutes this week and document your property:

  • Walk through your home with your phone and record a video of every room, opening closets and cabinets.
  • Photograph high-value items (electronics, appliances, furniture, tools).
  • Walk around the outside of your home and photograph the roof, siding, windows, and any outbuildings.
  • Store these files in cloud storage or email them to yourself so they are accessible even if your home is damaged.

This documentation is invaluable at claim time. It proves what you owned, what condition it was in, and what needs to be replaced. Without it, you are relying on memory, and memory does not hold up well in claims negotiations.

After the storm: what to do

We have a detailed guide on filing a home insurance claim after storm damage, but the short version:

  • Make sure everyone is safe.
  • Document the damage with photos and video before making temporary repairs.
  • Make temporary repairs to prevent further damage (tarp a damaged roof, board up broken windows). Your policy covers reasonable costs for temporary repairs.
  • Contact your insurance company to report the claim as soon as possible.
  • Do not sign anything from a door-to-door contractor without talking to your agent first.

    • If you have been through a storm before, you know how stressful the aftermath can be. Having your coverage in order before the storm makes the recovery process significantly smoother.

    Review your coverage now, not after the warning sounds

    The time to check your insurance is before you need it. Once a tornado watch is issued for your county, it is too late to make changes. Carriers will not bind new coverage or modify existing policies during an active weather event.
