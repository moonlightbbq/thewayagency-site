"Insurance for Nashville's Growing Small Business Scene"

By The Way Agency, Independent Insurance Agency · October 21, 2026 · 6 min read

Nashville has been one of the fastest-growing cities in the Southeast for years. New restaurants, creative agencies, tech startups, construction companies, and service businesses are opening every month. If you are starting or running a small business in Nashville, you need insurance that matches your risks — and Tennessee has its own requirements that differ from neighboring states.

We are an independent agency licensed in Tennessee, and we work with Nashville-area business owners every day. Here is what you should know about getting the right coverage for your Davidson County business.

Tennessee business insurance requirements

Tennessee law does not require most businesses to carry general liability insurance, but that does not mean you can skip it. Landlords, clients, and contracts almost always require proof of coverage before you can sign a lease or start a job.

Here is what Tennessee does require:

Workers' compensation. Tennessee requires workers' comp for any business with five or more employees (or one or more employees in the construction and mining industries). Penalties for non-compliance are steep — including fines and personal liability for workplace injuries.

Commercial auto insurance. If your business owns vehicles, Tennessee requires liability coverage with minimum limits of $25,000 per person, $50,000 per accident for bodily injury, and $15,000 for property damage. Most businesses should carry significantly more than the minimums.

Professional licensing requirements. Some professions in Tennessee — contractors, real estate agents, healthcare providers — require specific insurance as a condition of licensure.

The coverage most Nashville businesses need

Regardless of what the state requires, here is the insurance that most Nashville small businesses should carry:

General liability insurance is the foundation. It covers third-party bodily injury, property damage, and advertising injury claims. If a customer slips and falls in your shop, or your employee damages a client's property on a job site, general liability pays for the claim.

Commercial property insurance protects your physical assets — your building (if you own it), equipment, inventory, signage, and furniture. Nashville's severe thunderstorms and occasional tornado activity make this coverage essential.

Business income coverage replaces lost revenue if a covered event forces you to close temporarily. If your restaurant has a kitchen fire and you are shut down for two months, this coverage keeps your bills paid while you rebuild.

Workers' compensation covers medical costs and lost wages for employees injured on the job. Even if Tennessee does not require it for your business size, carrying it protects you from lawsuits if someone gets hurt at work.

Commercial auto insurance is necessary if you have company vehicles or if employees drive personal vehicles for business purposes (in which case, hired and non-owned auto coverage fills the gap).

Industry-specific risks in Nashville

Nashville's business landscape is diverse, and different industries face different risks:

Restaurants and bars. Nashville's food and entertainment scene is massive. If you serve alcohol, you need liquor liability insurance — Tennessee's dram shop laws can hold you liable if an intoxicated patron causes harm after leaving your establishment.

Construction and trades. Nashville's construction boom means contractors are in high demand. General contractors typically need general liability, workers' comp, commercial auto, inland marine (for tools and equipment), and an umbrella policy. Many general contractors require subcontractors to carry at least $1 million in general liability before they will hire them.

Creative and professional services. Nashville is home to thousands of creative professionals — designers, marketers, consultants, musicians, and tech companies. If your work involves professional advice or deliverables, errors and omissions (E&O) insurance protects you if a client claims your work caused them financial harm.

Retail and e-commerce. If you sell products — whether from a storefront on Broadway or an online shop — product liability coverage protects you if a product you sell injures someone or causes damage.

Healthcare and wellness. Nashville is a healthcare hub. Medical professionals, therapists, and wellness practitioners need professional liability (malpractice) insurance specific to their field.

Nashville-specific considerations

Severe weather. Nashville and Middle Tennessee experience significant severe weather, including tornadoes, straight-line winds, and flash flooding. The March 2020 tornado outbreak was a reminder of how quickly a storm can devastate a business. Make sure your commercial property coverage is adequate and consider business income coverage for downtime.

Flood risk. Parts of Nashville, particularly areas near the Cumberland River and its tributaries, are in flood zones. Standard commercial property insurance does not cover flooding. If your business is near water or in a low-lying area, talk to your agent about commercial flood insurance.

Growth and lease requirements. Nashville landlords, especially in high-demand areas like The Gulch, East Nashville, Germantown, and 12South, typically require tenants to carry at least $1 million in general liability and to name the landlord as an additional insured on the policy.

Metro Nashville business licenses. Davidson County requires a business license, and some license categories may trigger specific insurance requirements. Check with the Metro Clerk's Office to understand what applies to your business.

How much does small business insurance cost in Nashville?

Costs depend on your industry, revenue, employee count, and coverage needs. Here are rough ranges for common coverages:

  • General liability: $400 to $2,000 per year for most small businesses
  • Business Owner's Policy (BOP): $500 to $3,000 per year (bundles property and liability)
  • Workers' compensation: Varies by industry and payroll. Office-based businesses may pay $0.20 per $100 of payroll, while construction can be $5 to $15 per $100.
  • Professional liability (E&O): $500 to $3,000 per year depending on your profession and revenue
