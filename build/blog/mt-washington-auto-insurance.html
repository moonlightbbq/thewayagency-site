Auto Insurance for Mt. Washington Commuters: What I-65 Drivers Should Know

Reviewed by Jill Boone, Licensed Agent, The Way Agency | Published March 26, 2026 | 5 min read

Most people in Mt. Washington drive. Whether it is north on I-65 to Louisville, south toward Elizabethtown and Fort Knox, or around town on US-31E and KY-44, your daily driving patterns directly affect your auto insurance needs and what you should be paying.

Here is what Mt. Washington drivers should know about auto insurance in Kentucky, from minimum requirements to the coverage that actually protects you.

Kentucky auto insurance requirements

Kentucky is a choice no-fault state. That means every driver must carry:

  • Bodily injury liability: $25,000 per person / $50,000 per accident
  • Property damage liability: $25,000 per accident
  • Personal Injury Protection (PIP): $10,000

These are the legal minimums. They are also almost certainly not enough if you are involved in a serious accident on I-65.

Why minimums are risky for I-65 commuters

A highway accident at 70 mph is fundamentally different from a fender bender on Main Street. Medical bills from a serious interstate accident can reach six figures. If your liability limits are $25,000 per person and the other driver's injuries cost $150,000, you are personally responsible for the $125,000 difference.

For Mt. Washington residents who commute daily on I-65, we typically recommend:

  • 100/300/100 liability limits (or higher) instead of the 25/50/25 minimum
  • Uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage at matching limits
  • Comprehensive and collision if your vehicle has meaningful value
  • Medical payments coverage to supplement PIP

The cost difference between minimum and adequate coverage is often less than people expect. Higher liability limits might add $20 to $40 per month, but they protect your savings, your home equity, and your financial future.

Uninsured motorist coverage matters in Kentucky

Kentucky consistently has one of the highest rates of uninsured drivers in the country. That means there is a real chance that if you are hit by someone, they do not have insurance or do not have enough.

Uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) coverage pays for your injuries and damages when the other driver cannot. In a state with this many uninsured drivers, skipping UM/UIM coverage to save a few dollars a month is a gamble that does not make sense.

How to save on auto insurance in Mt. Washington

Price matters, and there are real ways to reduce your premium without cutting coverage:

  • Bundle home and auto: Most carriers offer a 5 to 25 percent multi-policy discount. If you are insuring a home and vehicles separately, bundling is the single easiest way to save.
  • Defensive driving course: Kentucky offers a premium discount for completing an approved defensive driving course. This is especially valuable for drivers over 55.
  • Higher deductible: Raising your comprehensive and collision deductible from $500 to $1,000 can save 10 to 15 percent on those coverages.
  • Shop multiple carriers: This is the biggest one. Different carriers price the same driver very differently. Because we are independent, we compare rates from dozens of carriers and find the best price for your specific driving profile.

Teenage drivers in Mt. Washington

If you have a teenage driver on your policy, your premium is going to increase. That is unavoidable. But there are ways to manage it:

  • Good student discount: Most carriers offer a discount for students maintaining a B average or better.
  • Driver training discount: Completing an approved driver education course qualifies for additional savings in Kentucky.
  • Usage-based programs: Some carriers offer telematics programs that reward safe driving habits. For a new driver, this can significantly offset the added premium.
